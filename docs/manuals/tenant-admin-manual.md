## Signing in (Story 6.1)

1. Go to `social-listening-admin`. If you're not already signed in, you're redirected to a sign-in page automatically.
2. Sign in with your organization's Microsoft Entra identity — the same real sign-in flow every SocialEngage identity uses.
3. Once signed in, SocialEngage recognizes your Tenant-Admin role and routes you to your own tenant's screens, never a System Admin's or another tenant's.
4. Your session stays signed in for up to 8 hours, then you'll be asked to sign in again automatically, even if you were actively using the app right up to that point — a deliberate security limit, not a bug.
5. To sign out, use the sign-out action — this ends your session immediately on this device.

## How you get your own tenant today

A self-service sign-up screen now exists and works end to end (Story 6.7) — entering your organization's name and signing in with Microsoft creates a brand-new tenant with you as its first Tenant-Admin, a real, persisted tenant, not a preview. That said, per this project's own current stage (`docs/project docs/Project Management Plans/Go-Live-Readiness-Definition.md` — Stage 0; no persistent environment or public onboarding is open yet), this path isn't open to the general public today. In practice, a tenant and its first Tenant-Admin are still typically set up by a Platform Admin directly, rather than through public self-service sign-up.

## Your tenant's own screens (Story 6.2)

Once you're signed in, SocialEngage shows you your own tenant's shell — screens for connecting platforms and turning them on or off, managing your own watchlists, checking connector status, managing your tenant's users, viewing your tenant's own settings, browsing your tenant's ingested posts, replying to a Facebook post (Story 6.38), drafting cross-platform posts (Story 6.36), searching your posts semantically or asking an AI assistant about them (Stories 9.7–9.11), and the Analytics dashboard. As a Tenant-Admin only, you also get same-domain sign-up review, your own company domain's content feed (Tenant-Admin only as of Story 6.20), per-user access history, and requesting deletion of the entire tenant. You'll never see another tenant's data or the System Admin area from your own sign-in.

## Your tenant's workspace overview (Story 6.2, visually refreshed 2026-08-20)

Signing in takes you to a "Workspace Overview" landing page — this section names it directly since it was never previously described in this manual, even though it's real and has existed since Story 6.2.

1. Four real metric cards — Active Watchlists, Ingestion Connectors, Ingested Posts, and Seat Utilization — each a live count from your tenant's own data, and each links straight to the fuller screen it summarizes.
2. If any connector is currently degraded or failing, a warning banner names it and links straight to the connector status screen.
3. A "Recent Ingestion Stream" shows your tenant's most recently ingested posts, newest first, and an "Active Connectors" list on the side shows which platforms are currently active.
4. As a Tenant-Admin, you also get quick-action buttons here ("+ New Watchlist," "Tenant-wide connect").

## Getting-started checklist (Story 9.6, role-tailored journeys added by Story 17.2)

The Workspace Overview page (see above) also shows a "Get Started with SocialEngage" checklist card whenever your tenant has one — both a Tenant-Admin and a Tenant User see it.

1. Four core steps — Connect an Ingestion Source, Create a Brand Watchlist, Invite a Team Member, Verify Ingested Posts — each shown with a checkmark once genuinely completed, a plain description, and a link straight to the screen where you'd do it. A "Show Advanced Setup Steps" toggle reveals two more: Enable AI Enrichment and Configure Crisis Monitoring. A progress percentage and a filled bar at the top of the card reflect your tenant's real, computed completion state, not a guess.
2. **As of Story 17.2, the card also offers four role tabs — Admin, Care Agent, Social Seller, and Brand Manager** — each switching to its own tailored set of steps and links relevant to that way of using SocialEngage, with the same real, computed completion checkmarks and a percentage specific to that role's own steps, alongside the original core checklist under "Overview."
3. As a Tenant-Admin, clicking "Dismiss ✕" hides the card and this is remembered for your tenant going forward (a "Show Setup Checklist" button reappears in its place if you want it back, and reopening is remembered the same way).
4. **Current limitation:** if a Tenant User (not a Tenant-Admin) dismisses the checklist, that dismissal is only remembered for their own current browser session — it isn't saved to your tenant's record, so it reappears for that Tenant User the next time they load the page. Only a Tenant-Admin's dismissal persists.

## Semantic search and an AI assistant over your posts — Discovery (Stories 9.7–9.11)

Open "Discovery" from your tenant's screens to search your tenant's ingested posts by meaning rather than exact keywords, or ask a natural-language question and get a generated answer grounded in your own posts. Both Tenant-Admins and Tenant Users can use this screen; there's no role difference on it.

1. A status indicator at the top shows how many post chunks are currently indexed for semantic search, and a green or amber dot for whether indexing is healthy.
2. Two modes, switched with a tab: **Semantic Search** (returns a ranked list of matching post snippets, each with a percentage match badge and a link back to that post in your Posts feed) and **Ask AI Assistant** (returns a written answer with numbered citation markers, each backed by a real indexed post snippet shown in a citations rail alongside the answer, plus a plain-language confidence label). Press `Cmd/Ctrl+K` at any time to jump straight to the search box.
3. You can narrow either mode by Platform, Watchlist, or Sentiment before searching or asking.
4. If your tenant's own indexed posts don't actually contain enough to answer a question, the Ask assistant tells you so directly in an amber callout rather than inventing an answer — it never fabricates a citation.

**Current limitation, real and not yet resolved:** a post is only searchable or citable here once it has been indexed into this feature's own separate vector index — indexing is not yet wired into live ingestion itself (Stories 9.8/2.32's Deep Research feature and this feature use separate pipelines), so a newly ingested post won't show up in Discovery until an operator has run the backend indexing/backfill process. In practice this means Discovery's coverage of "your posts" can lag behind what the Posts feed already shows.

## Connecting a platform (Story 6.3)

1. From your tenant's screens, open "Connect a platform."
2. You'll see every platform SocialEngage currently supports as its own card — GNews, Newswire, Azure AI Language, Azure OpenAI Service, Wikipedia (Story 6.21), Brave Search (Story 6.30), Bing Search/Azure (Story 6.32), and YouTube Data API (Story 10.13) — each shown with its real connection state (Connected, along with the credential's own status such as valid, expiring soon, expired, or revoked, or Not connected) and whether it's currently Active or Inactive. Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn are also shown here, but work differently — see "Connecting your own social accounts" below.
3. Before you submit any credential, the screen states plainly that you're creating your own account or API key directly with that provider, under that provider's own terms — SocialEngage is not signing you up with the provider and is not a billing intermediary for it.
4. To connect GNews, Azure AI Language, Azure OpenAI Service, Brave Search, Bing Search, or YouTube Data API, enter the credential fields that platform needs (a single API key for GNews, Brave Search, Bing Search, or YouTube Data API; an endpoint, key, and region for Azure AI Language; an endpoint, deployment name, and key for Azure OpenAI Service) and submit. Newswire and Wikipedia need no credential at all — there's nothing to submit, and both always show as connected.
5. **Scope varies by platform, not just by whether you're a Tenant-Admin.** GNews is the only platform that offers you a choice: as a Tenant-Admin connecting GNews, you can choose whether the new connection is "Tenant-wide" (shared by everyone in your tenant) or "Just for me" (a personal connection only you can use); as a Tenant User, connecting GNews always creates a personal connection just for you. Azure AI Language, Azure OpenAI Service, Brave Search, Bing Search, and YouTube Data API are always tenant-wide only — Azure, Brave/Bing Search, and YouTube API keys authenticate your organization's own subscription, not an individual's personal account, so no personal option is offered for any of the five, even to a Tenant-Admin; a Tenant User who isn't a Tenant-Admin and finds one of these not yet connected sees a plain note asking them to have their Tenant-Admin connect it, rather than a button that would just fail. Newswire and Wikipedia need no credential and so have no scope choice either. Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn are the opposite — always personal, never tenant-wide, for anyone; see below.
6. Disconnecting asks you to confirm before it actually removes the connection — it's a two-step "Disconnect," then "Confirm: disconnect," never a single click, and it's immediate.
7. Alongside connect/disconnect, each platform also has its own Activate/Deactivate control (Story 6.15) — a separate switch from the credential itself. Deactivating a platform keeps its stored credential in place (so you can reactivate it later without reconnecting) but stops it from being used for ingestion. A newly connected platform, and Newswire in particular, is not automatically Active — a Tenant-Admin has to explicitly turn it on.

**Current limitation:** the personal ("Just for me") Activate/Deactivate control always starts by showing Inactive, even if you'd already activated it before — there's no way yet for this screen to read back your own real personal on/off state; only the tenant-wide state can be read today. If it's actually already on, clicking Activate again is harmless.

## Connecting your own social accounts — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn (Stories 6.23, 6.27, 6.34, 6.35)

Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn work differently from every other platform on the Connect a platform screen — each uses a real sign-in redirect (OAuth) instead of an API key, and all three are personal to you, and only you, with no "Tenant-wide" option, for anyone, including a Tenant-Admin.

1. Click that platform's own "Connect" button. You're taken to a real sign-in and permission screen on that provider's own site, not a form inside SocialEngage.
2. **Facebook (Story 6.23, extended by Story 6.27 to support more than one Page):** after you approve it, you're brought back and shown checkboxes for every Facebook Page you're an admin of — you can select and connect more than one Page at once, not just a single one as this connector originally allowed. Each connected Page gets its own row with its own real health, and its own "Reconnect" or "Disconnect" action — disconnecting one Page doesn't touch any others you've connected. If SocialEngage's access to a Page is later lost (for example, you remove SocialEngage's access on Facebook's own side, or lose admin rights to that Page), that row is marked "Access lost — reconnect to restore this Page" rather than showing a generic failure. "Connect another Page" is always available to add more later. Facebook posts are never ingested from public content, other people's posts, or comments — only your own connected Page's own published posts and engagement.
3. **Instagram Business (Story 6.34):** connects an Instagram Business or Creator account the same way, via Meta's Graph API. Only your own connected account's own published photos, videos, carousels, and reels are ingested.
4. **LinkedIn (Story 6.35):** connects your own LinkedIn member account. If LinkedIn hasn't yet approved this app for organization-level (company page) access, a plain callout banner says so ("Organization features unavailable — partner scope approval pending") without blocking ingestion of your own member-level posts.
5. Turning any of the three on or off uses the same Activate/Deactivate control every other platform has.
6. If a provider ever revokes your connection (for example, you remove SocialEngage's access on that provider's own side), the affected card shows a distinct "Reconnect required" status rather than a generic failure — click "Reconnect" to go through the sign-in flow again.

## Managing watchlists (Story 6.4, boolean query builder added by Stories 12.3/12.4, volume/cost preview added by Story 18.1)

1. From your tenant's screens, open "Manage watchlists."
2. You'll see your own watchlists — name, match type (keyword, hashtag, account, or boolean), whether each is active, and which connected platforms it covers. **Watchlists are private to you** — even as a Tenant-Admin, you can't see or manage another user's watchlists from this screen; there is no tenant-wide oversight view of everyone's watchlists.
3. Below the list, a form lets you create a new watchlist: a name, a match type, either search terms (one per line) or — for a boolean watchlist — a query built with a visual query builder, and which of your connected platforms it should cover. Only platforms you've actually connected (GNews, Newswire, Wikipedia, Brave Search, and/or Bing Search, since Stories 6.22, 6.30, and 6.32) are offered here; the two AI enrichment providers never appear as watchlist platforms, since a watchlist matches ingested posts by the source platform they came from, not by which AI provider might later analyze them. Your own company domain's content feed and your connected Facebook/Instagram/LinkedIn accounts aren't offered here yet either — a real, still-open gap, not a deliberate exclusion.
4. **The boolean query builder (Stories 12.3/12.4)** lets you build a query as guided blocks (keyword, phrase, hashtag, mention, author, source, sentiment, or date clauses joined with AND/OR/NOT and groups) instead of typing raw boolean text, with a toggle to switch to a plain text editor for the same query. As you build it, the screen checks your query against what each platform you've selected can actually evaluate and warns you before you save if a clause isn't supported everywhere.
5. Each watchlist can be edited the same way it was created, switched active/inactive with its own dedicated toggle, and deleted. Deleting asks for a separate confirm step ("Delete," then "Confirm delete") before anything is actually removed, and it's permanent.
6. **When editing an existing watchlist, a "📊 Preview volume" button (Story 18.1)** estimates how many posts your current query would actually match — either an exact count or a labeled sample estimate, depending on the connector — along with a rough cost/quota indicator, and flags it if the projected volume is unusually high or would consume a large share of your remaining quota. This preview isn't offered yet while first creating a new watchlist, only once it already exists.
7. If a watchlist has changed elsewhere since you loaded the page (for example, edited in a second browser tab), saving your own change won't silently overwrite the other one — you'll see a message telling you it changed elsewhere and asking you to reload the page before retrying.

### Crisis Threshold Wizard (Story 9.4)

An "⚡ Crisis Wizard" button on the Manage watchlists screen (also offered from the empty-watchlists state) opens a 3-step wizard for activating a standardized reputation-risk monitoring template rather than building a watchlist by hand.

1. **Step 1 — Select a template.** Choose from a small set of pre-built scenarios (for example, Brand Crisis, Product Recall, Executive Attack, Competitor Surge, Data Breach), each with a plain description and a severity badge.
2. **Step 2 — Variables.** Fill in the specific names or keywords the template needs (for example, your own brand name or product name) — a live preview shows the exact search query your values will produce. Required fields are marked, and the wizard won't let you continue past a missing one.
3. **Step 3 — Thresholds & playbook.** Adjust the volume-spike and negative-sentiment percentages that would flag this as a crisis (sensible defaults are pre-filled), and review the template's own advisory response playbook — a numbered list of response steps, each with a named owner role and an SLA in minutes, shown for reference only.
4. Clicking "⚡ Activate Crisis Watchlist" creates a real, active watchlist immediately from your selections — this is a real, persisted watchlist just like one you'd build by hand, private to you like every other watchlist.

**Current limitation, disclosed directly in the wizard itself:** activating a template creates a real monitoring watchlist right away, but automated alert dispatch (email, Slack, or webhook) for a crisis threshold being crossed is not built yet — the wizard's own banner says this plainly ("will be enabled in an upcoming release").

## Checking connector status (Story 6.5, restructured by Stories 6.24 and 6.29)

1. From your tenant's screens, open "View connector status."
2. **As of Story 6.24, this screen is split into two sections: "Connectors" and "AI Providers."** GNews, Newswire, Wikipedia, Facebook, Brave Search, Bing Search, Instagram, and LinkedIn — every platform that's actually polled for content — appear under "Connectors," each with whether it's Active or Inactive, and if Active, its real health, the time of its last successful fetch (now shown together with how many posts that run ingested, Story 6.5 telemetry enhancement), the time of its last attempt, its consecutive-failure count, and its own real polling interval. Azure AI Language and Azure OpenAI Service appear separately, under "AI Providers," with a plain note explaining they're invoked on demand while a post is being processed, not polled on a schedule — this corrects an earlier, real display bug where an AI provider that was actually connected and working could show as "Paused" next to a Deactivate button, because the connector-status logic never distinguished the two kinds of platform.
3. **A connector can now show a "Stalled" status (Story 1.16, Story 6.29)** — distinct from "Failing" — when it hasn't successfully polled in a while even though it isn't actively erroring, for example because a scheduled run never completed. A dismissible banner at the top of this screen (and on the Analytics dashboard) lists every connector currently Stalled, Failing, or needing reconnection, with a direct action for each.
4. **As a Tenant-Admin, an active, unhealthy connector's card also has a "Re-sync now" button** (Story 6.29) that asks SocialEngage to attempt an ingestion run for that connector right now, rather than waiting for its next scheduled poll. If a run is already in progress, you'll see a plain "already in progress" message rather than a duplicate run being started.
5. As a Tenant-Admin, you also get the same Activate/Deactivate control here that's available from the Connect a platform screen.
6. Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn each show a distinct "Reconnect required" status here too, with its own "Reconnect" action, whenever that provider itself has revoked your connection — the same signal the Connect a platform screen shows.
7. This screen shows status only — never your posts or any other tenant content.

**Current limitations:**
- Watchlist compatibility warnings (which boolean-query features a given connector can't natively evaluate) are not shown here yet — the screen says so directly rather than showing fake data. Surfacing this needs a new backend endpoint that doesn't exist yet.
- The polling-interval and consecutive-failure-ceiling figures shown per connector are drawn from a small, hand-maintained list in the screen's own code, not read live from the backend — accurate as of the connectors that exist today, but not automatically guaranteed to stay in sync if a connector's own cadence ever changes.

## Managing your tenant's users — Team & access (Story 6.8, redesigned 2026-08-17)

1. From your tenant's screens, open "Team & access." Every user in your tenant — Tenant-Admin or Tenant User — can see this list: email, role, status, and when their access ends (shown as "active indefinitely" if no end date is set). ## Browsing your tenant's posts (Story 6.11, updated by Stories 6.18, 6.19, 6.25, 6.26, 6.33, 6.37, 12.5/12.6)

Once you've connected and activated a platform, SocialEngage now polls it automatically in the background — GNews, Newswire, and Wikipedia every 15–30 minutes, your own domain's content feed every 30 minutes (Story 1.13), and your own connected Facebook Page every 30 minutes (Story 1.15) — so posts appear here on their own, with nothing you need to click to make ingestion happen. (Before this scheduler shipped, on 2026-08-13, there was no automatic ingestion in this app's real running instance at all; and before Story 1.15 specifically, on 2026-08-18, a connected Facebook Page was never actually polled at all despite showing as connected — only what's described here reflects real, live behavior.)

1. From your tenant's screens, open "Posts" to see what SocialEngage has ingested for your tenant — **newest-ingested first** (Story 6.25), each with the platform it came from, the author where known, a title and body preview, and its published date. If a post has already been analyzed by an AI provider, you'll also see its sentiment, key phrases, entities, and the detected language right in the list, along with up to three entity and three key-phrase chips.
2. A search box and a set of filters — Provider, Sentiment, and Watchlist — sit above the list. **As of Story 6.18, search and every filter operate over your tenant's entire ingested history, not just the current page** — before that story shipped, they only ever searched the current 20-post page, which was a real, confirmed gap. The Provider filter (Story 6.26) automatically lists every platform your tenant actually has posts from, including every connector that's shipped since, without needing to be updated by hand as new connectors ship.
3. Below the list, a "Show more" button reveals more of your already-filtered result set in batches — there's no separate page-number picker or server round trip for paging; everything you've searched or filtered has already been fetched.
4. Click any post to open a detail panel on the same screen (a slide-over, not a separate page) showing the same information plus the full article body — rendered as real, formatted Markdown, not raw HTML or a plain-text snippet (Story 6.19) — the author (currently shown as a raw internal identifier — there's no lookup yet to turn it into a friendlier name) and which ingestion run brought it in (also a raw identifier, for the same reason). A direct link to a single post's own page (`/tenant/posts/:id`) still exists and shows the same detail, but nothing in the Posts screen itself links to it — opening a post always uses the slide-over panel.
5. **A post from Facebook shows which of your connected Pages it came from** (Story 6.33), and, separately from that, its own real author when Facebook reports one distinct from the Page itself — a "By: <author>" line only appears when the two differ, since most Page posts are published as the Page. **Any post matched by one of your watchlists shows that watchlist's name** in the card and detail view too (Stories 6.37, 3.11–3.12), reflecting the real, persisted link between a post and the watchlist(s) that matched it, not a guess made after the fact. An Instagram post shows its own media type and a thumbnail, with a gallery for a multi-image carousel; a LinkedIn post shows its own reaction/comment/share counts.
6. **When a post's AI analysis breaks sentiment down by aspect (Story 12.5/12.6)** — for example, "service: negative, price: positive" for a review that liked the price but not the service — the detail panel shows an "Aspect-Level Sentiment Breakdown" list underneath the overall sentiment badge. Not every post has this; it only appears when the enrichment provider actually detected distinct aspects.

## Replying to a post (Stories 2.26, 2.27, 3.14, 6.38)

1. On a post's detail panel, next to the "Details" tab, there's a "Replies" tab and, alongside it, a "Reply" button. Today the "Reply" button is only ever enabled for a post from Facebook, and only when you personally have a connected, active Facebook Page credential — for any other platform, or if you haven't connected your own Facebook Page, the button is disabled with a tooltip explaining why.
2. Clicking "Reply" opens a composer drawer next to the post detail panel. Write your reply (up to Facebook's own character limit, shown live as you type) and click "Send Reply."
3. **Sending a reply is real, not simulated** — unlike the cross-platform post composer's "Publish" action, this actually posts a live comment to that post on Facebook through Facebook's own API, immediately, with no draft, review, or scheduling step.
4. Whether it succeeded or failed, you'll see a toast, and the attempt is added to that post's own "Replies" tab, showing its status (sent or failed), when it happened, and — for a sent reply — a "View live reply" link straight to the real comment on Facebook.
5. Every reply you send is recorded permanently against that post, whether it succeeded or failed, so the Replies tab is a genuine audit trail, not just a live status indicator.

**Current limitations:** replying only works for Facebook today — no other platform's connector supports it yet, so the button stays disabled everywhere else. If you have more than one of your own Facebook Pages connected (Story 6.27), replying currently uses whichever Page you connected most recently, not necessarily the Page the post actually came from — if those two differ, Facebook will reject the reply with a permission error. The reply composer also has no media attachment or AI-rewrite option, unlike the full cross-platform post composer.

## Running enrichment manually, and correcting it by hand (Story 6.16, extended by Stories 3.13 and 6.31)

1. On a post's detail screen, if that post has never been analyzed by an AI provider, you'll see a "Run enrichment now" button — this typically applies to a post that was ingested before your tenant had connected and activated any AI provider.
2. Clicking it asks SocialEngage to analyze that one post right now, using whichever AI provider your tenant currently has connected and active. On success, the screen reloads and shows the real sentiment, key phrases, entities, and which provider produced them.
3. If no AI provider is currently connected and active for your tenant, you'll see a plain message saying so rather than an error — connect and activate one first (see "Connecting a platform" above) if you want to enrich posts this way.
4. **As of Story 6.31, a post that has been analyzed also has an "Edit" button** on its AI analysis card. It opens a second panel, alongside the post detail panel rather than replacing it, letting you correct the AI's own sentiment (a Positive/Neutral/Negative choice), key phrases (add or remove, up to 50), detected language, country, and summary by hand. Saving records your correction immediately and marks the post with an amber "Edited by user" badge, which shows who made the change and when if you hover over it — your correction is never silently blended back into what the AI originally produced.
5. Once a post has been manually corrected this way, clicking "Run enrichment now" again asks you to confirm first, since re-running would otherwise overwrite your own correction with a fresh AI result — confirming replaces it (keeping the prior AI and human values on record for that post), while backing out leaves your correction exactly as you left it.
6. This "Run enrichment now" button only appears on a post with no enrichment yet, or after you've explicitly confirmed you want to re-run it on a corrected one — it's never available to silently overwrite a correction.

## Monitoring your own company domain's content feed — now a multi-feed admin screen (Stories 6.12, 6.17, 6.20, 6.28)

If you want SocialEngage to monitor your own company blog or newsroom rather than a third-party platform, this is a separate flow from "Connect a platform," with no credential to submit — instead, you prove you control the domain.

**Corrected 2026-08-17 (Story 6.20): this entire screen is now Tenant-Admin only.** Earlier (Stories 6.12/6.17), a Tenant User could set up and verify a feed, with only activation reserved for a Tenant-Admin. Story 6.20 tightened this — connecting, editing, removing, and viewing your tenant's configured feeds now all require a Tenant-Admin session on the backend itself, not just on the button that starts the flow. A Tenant User can still open this screen (they're not redirected away), but sees an empty feed list and no working actions, since the list itself is now Tenant-Admin-only data.

1. As a Tenant-Admin, from "Connect a platform," follow "Configure Custom Domain Feed" (or go directly to "Monitor your own domain's content feed"). You'll see every feed your tenant has already configured, each with its domain, feed URL, and status (pending verification, verified, or inactive).
2. To add another one, click "Connect another feed," enter the domain and the feed URL (your blog or newsroom's RSS/Atom feed), optionally give it a friendly name (Story 6.28 — for example, "Company Blog" instead of the raw domain), and submit. A feed with a name shows that name as its main label, with the domain shown underneath it; a feed with no name just shows its domain, as before. A name can be added or changed later without re-verifying the feed. If your tenant has already verified that exact domain for a different feed, the new one is verified immediately — you don't need to repeat the DNS proof for a domain you've already proven you control.
3. Otherwise, SocialEngage gives you a DNS TXT record (a host, a value, and an expiry) to publish at your own domain registrar. Publishing it can take anywhere from a few minutes up to 72 hours to take effect — this is normal DNS propagation, not a stuck or broken state. **As of Story 6.20, these DNS details stay visible on this screen for as long as the feed is still pending** — leaving and returning, or reloading the page, no longer loses them.
4. Once you've published the record, click "Verify now" on that feed's own row. If it's not detected yet, you'll see a plain message to try again shortly — this isn't an error, and you can click "Verify now" as many times as you need.
5. A verified feed can be edited (its feed URL only — its domain can't be changed) or removed at any time. Removing a feed stops it from being polled but keeps everything it already ingested.
6. A verified feed still isn't monitored on its own — a single Activate/Deactivate control at the bottom of the screen turns ingestion on or off for every feed your tenant has configured together (there's no per-feed on/off switch). If you have verified feeds but this switch is off, the screen tells you plainly that nothing is currently being polled.

**Current limitation:** this connector still has no personal, "just for me" option at all — every feed is tenant-wide, and, as of Story 6.20, only a Tenant-Admin can see or manage any of it.

## Deleting your tenant (Story 6.13)

This is the one truly irreversible action available anywhere in SocialEngage — read this section in full before starting. **This screen and everything in it is Tenant-Admin only** — a Tenant User has no entry point to it anywhere, not even a hidden or greyed-out one; going to `/tenant/settings/delete` directly redirects them back to the ordinary tenant settings screen. There is also no link to this screen from the ordinary Tenant settings screen (Story 6.9) today — you currently have to navigate to it directly.

1. Open the tenant deletion screen and choose "Request deletion." This starts a 30-day grace period — nothing is deleted yet, and your tenant keeps working normally.
2. During the grace period, you'll see the date your tenant will actually be deleted unless you cancel before then. You can export your tenant's data as JSON or CSV any number of times before that date.
3. To change your mind, choose "Cancel deletion request," then confirm — ingestion resumes immediately and your tenant carries on as if nothing happened.
4. Once the 30-day grace period has actually elapsed, a "Confirm deletion" button becomes available (it's disabled, with a plain explanation, before then). Confirming asks you to confirm a second time, with an explicit warning that this is final and cannot be undone, and that all of your tenant's data — posts, watchlists, and credentials — will be permanently deleted.
5. After you confirm, deletion runs in the background. The screen shows a plain "in progress" message and does not update further — your own session will simply stop working once deletion actually completes.

**Current limitation:** if a deletion request is already active when you open this screen, there's no way for the screen itself to tell you exactly when the grace period ends (only the moment you first request deletion shows that date) — the "Confirm deletion" button still correctly stays disabled until the backend itself confirms the grace period has elapsed, so you can't accidentally jump ahead, but the countdown display itself may be missing on a return visit.
